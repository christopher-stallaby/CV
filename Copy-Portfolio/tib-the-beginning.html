The Beginning

“For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” - Jesus: Matthew 12:34b (ESV) If you want to know what is in the heart or mind of God on a subject, you can never go wrong with reading Scripture to see what He has said about it.

I remember one encounter I had with Jesus in which I was saying to Him, “I want to know Your heart.” I kept repeating this phrase again and again, the intensity of desire growing with each repetition. Softly His response came, “Read My Word.” Immediately the Scripture Matthew 12:34 came to mind where Jesus says, “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” I’ve been on a journey to discover what God thinks about technology. So I’ve been listening to the Bible and consolidating Scriptures that refer to technology. I’m using this blog to think through what God says in Scripture in order to better understand His mind. I’d like to invite you to join me on this journey into the mind of God to discover what He thinks about technology. Before I progress further with this endeavor though, I should define technology. When this term is used in popular culture, it generally refers to digital devices of some sort; mobile phones, laptops, desktops, tablets, or their accessories like bluetooth mouses, headphones or watches. However, in the course of this blog I will use technology to refer to anything that is invented by humans with the intent to improve the quality of human life or increase the capabilities of human efforts. Now, let’s begin. The first invention recorded in the Bible is seen in Genesis 3:7. Adam and Eve, in reaction to their sin and shame create clothes for themselves out of fig leaves. Fig leaves contain skin irritants which would make wearing them uncomfortable for the wearer. So on one hand Adam and Eve were covering themselves and on another hand, they were punishing themselves. This, though an interesting point is not a comment on technology. It could be said here that the creation of technology is a response to the fall and the presence of sin in the world. Another point is that technology can often be man’s attempt to escape or correct the ill effects of the fall. Does this mean that technology then is evil since it is a human attempt to undo sin and the fall? Not necessarily, as we see in Genesis 3:21 that God Himself upgrades the clothes that Adam and Eve had made for themselves. He killed a lamb and from its skin fashioned clothes that would adequately cover the man and woman specifically. These lamb skins would be much more comfortable than the fig leaves they were wearing previously. So God affirms their need for covering, and creates for them a comfortable form of clothing. The clothes God makes for them are better in many ways, not only in the level of comfort. Leaves cut off from the tree will quickly wither, turn brown and fall apart. The lambs skin clothes would last indefinitely. What we see here is that God participates in the technology that man created in an attempt to cover his mistake. He upgrades the technology, making it better, and He also makes it redemptive; as the lamb He kills points to the Perfect Lamb, His Son. So technology isn’t something God seems to want to do away with or reverse or undo from human history. Instead He uses human technology to His redemptive purposes. When He does this, the technology is improved for human experience. This is only the beginning of our journey and there is much more discovery to come. Next time we will pick up with the story of Cain and Abel and see how technology is continually woven throughout the Biblical narrative to reveal God’s desires for tech.
