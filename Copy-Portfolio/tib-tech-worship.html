Technological Worship

[← In the beginning, we saw that God participates with humanity in the inception and iteration of the technologies we create. Now we shall see that God teaches men how to create new technologies and when God and men are in right relationship, the results will be worship for God and goodness for the entire human race.

Cain and Abel are the first human beings recorded as born after the fall. What is the first course action we see these first natural born humans take? Genesis 4 briefly describes their trades, Cain a farmer and Abel a shepherd; the first action each man is described to enact though is that of offering the fruit of their labor to God in thanks. Adam was cursed so that the ground would only produce thorns and thistles for all his effort, Genesis 3:17-19. His son Cain however learned to produce all manner of fruits and vegetables from the ground out of which his father Adam could only produce thorns. Where did Cain gain this knowledge? Isaiah 28:26 gives the answer, “The farmer knows just what to do, for God has given him understanding.” So God gave Cain special knowledge such that he was able to bring life from that which had only given death to Adam. Since Cain was taught by God, we can assume that Abel was also taught by God how to breed his flock so that it prospered and grew. What then was the response from these men who learned how to conduct their trades from the Creator? Genesis 4:4 shows us that each man chose to worship God by bringing an offering from the products of their trades. New technologies of agriculture and livestock husbandry resulted in worship for God. Two experts in their fields chose to offer to God that which He had taught them to craft. These two men, understanding they were taught by God, responded with worship to God. Another similar instance can be found in Genesis 8:20-22. Noah receives a blueprint and instructions for creating a new mortar-like material called pitch, from God. He builds the ark to the specifications. With the new material and he and his family, along with the animals, survive the flood. His reaction when he exits the ark is to offer to God a clean animal on an altar which he built. This is the first time we see “altar” mentioned in Scripture. The construction of altars came about because of advancements in technology that came from God. From these examples in Scripture it is plain that God teaches humanity how to advance technology. It would seem that righteousness is not necessarily a requirement for this learning, as Cain is taught by God as well as Abel and Noah. When man believes these technological breakthroughs come from his own mind and the power of his intellect pride and destruction will result as it did for Cain. Yet even in the destruction, there is a redemptive plan God weaves with technology, throughout human history.
