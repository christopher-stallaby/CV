Innovations Galore

In Genesis 4:8, Cain attacks and kills his brother Abel. Only 9 verses later in verse 17 Cain founds a city and names it Enoch, after his son. The Bible compresses at least a year’s worth of time into these 8 verses and gives us no detail into those days beyond the initial conversation between God and Cain.

Obviously Cain has undergone a transformation during this time. He learns to live under the anger of God, bearing the mark of vengeance that God placed on him to warn anyone who might kill him. Cain leaves Eden and settles elsewhere, marries and has a son. According to Rabbi Daniel Lapin, this is evidence that Cain learned his lesson. He killed his brother out of greed, believing that his brother would limit his ability to gain. After God’s judgement he marries and founds a city, a place for many people to share ownership and settle together. Again the Bible compresses time, this time skipping 6 generations. Only names are given for Cain’s children until Jabal, Jubal, Tubal-cain and their sister Naamah come along and insights into their lives are shared. Presumably in the city established by Cain, musicians and metal-smiths arose. Creatives from a community of people, expressing their God-given creativity and making life easier and more enjoyable for everyone. Jabal is credited as the father of all those who raise livestock and live in tents (Genesis 4:20). His brother Jubal became the first of all who play the harp and flute (Genesis 4:21). Tubal-cain, half-brother to Jabal and Jubal, became an expert in forging tools of bronze and iron (Genesis 4:22). Tony Reinke in his book, *God Technology and The Christian Life* theorizes that Naamah’s mention in Scripture along with her brothers indicates a woman of special note. He postulates that she was a singer whose voice accompanied her half brother Jubal’s skill with instruments. Whatever the case may be with Naamah’s special inclusion in the genealogy one thing is clear, God in His infinite wisdom and foresight, allowed these descendants of Cain’s to arise and [as He did with both Cain and Abel](https://www.notion.so/Technological-Worship-1ca16ece5cdb802690c4c03975784bbf?pvs=21), taught them their specific skills. Since we know the nature of God to be good, we may safely assume that God has an redemptive reason for allowing these advancements in technology. God is not fearful of the innovations that humans discover and invent, rather He is the source from whom all innovations arise. He teaches those who are skillful in their work, expanding the limits of human knowledge and granting us understanding of His world in order to assist us in our original mission to “subdue” the earth (Genesis 1:28). So then God is not an inactive observer of the creative process by which new inventions are brought into the world, nor is He threatened by the latest most cutting edge advancements that are discovered. Instead He, like a loving Father [leads us into each discovery](https://www.notion.so/The-Beginning-19416ece5cdb808a9d27d0ce95a27a99?pvs=21), granting to us greater measures of His infinite knowledge and placing upon us the responsibility to use it for redemptive purposes. There is nothing that exists that God is not capable of harnessing for His ultimate glory and our greatest good. From the iPhone, to the engines of war and destruction, to the healing technologies in medicinal practice, to those intended to restore the planet, each invention and innovation is discovered by humanity because God teaches us. His purposes for each invention shall stand in the end. Even weapons, Scripture tells us serve a purpose in God’s perfect world. Isaiah 2:4, “They will hammer their swords into plowshares and their spears into pruning hooks.” Ezekiel 39:9 “Then the people in the towns of Israel will go out and pick up your small and large shields, bows and arrows, javelins and spears, and they will use them for fuel. There will be enough to last them seven years!” Weapons of war will be transformed into methods of production and provision for the people of God. The Christian need not fear technological advancements for none of them are outside God’s intentions to redeem His creation.
