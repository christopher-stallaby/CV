Group Lead Notes

Remember Me

Matthew 26 is a chapter that begins solemnly with Jesus prophesying about His coming death at the hands of sinful men...

We walk with the disciples through the anointing of Jesus by Mary the woman who had been a prostitute and Jesus says again that His death is coming shortly. As He keeps the Passover feast with the twelve and Judas leaves to betray Him to the High Priest and turn Him over to them, we are invited to gaze at the supper table as they eat and Jesus does something new. Matthew 26:26-28 “As they were eating, Jesus took the bread, blessed and broke it, gave it to the disciples, and said, ‘Take and eat it; this is My body.’ Then He took a cup, and after giving thanks, He gave it to them and said, ‘Drink from it, all of you. For this is My blood that establishes the covenant; it is shed for many for the forgiveness of sins.” This is a beautiful scene, differing from our usual Communion in a few aspects. They were sitting around a table altogether in close quarters, in the Gospel of John we are told that when John reclined he didn’t have a chair that he was sitting in but that he was sitting on the floor next to Jesus and leaned against Him. Unlike our usual Communion instances where we are separated into rows and columns of people. Their experience was personal, intimate. When Jesus broke the bread, He took flourless, flat, roasted bread and separated it into two pieces and gave them to the person on His right and on His left. The disciples each took their portion, broke a piece off for themselves, then passed the bread along. For the cup, He took the wine and poured it into one cup and gave it to one of them who took his drink and passed the cup along to the next man. Each aspect of this dinner points to the sacrifice, the crucifixion of Jesus. From beginning to end there is significance to the food and the wine, each being symbolic of something that would happen to Jesus. When Jesus broke the bread first He was pointing directly to His statement that no one takes His life from Him, but He freely lays it down. Again with Him pouring out the wine then giving them to drink He reiterates that He has chosen to lay down His life, no one forced Him, no one coerced Him, He was not negotiated or convinced into this. His sacrifice is an unsolicited act of His will which is targeted for our benefit. He then turned over His body into their hands. They broke off pieces of bread signifying what Jesus had told His disciples at the beginning of this chapter, which was probably a week or so earlier from their point of view, that He would be turned over to the hands of sinful men to be killed. This shows that not only did He break His body, but we broke His body as well. As they ate the bread, their teeth and saliva broke the bread even further and yet the food added strength and sustenance to their bodies. The body that was broken by us provided salvation for us. We broke His body because He surrendered His body to us to be broken. The very body that we broke became the source of our salvation. He poured out His blood to be consumed by His disciples. The whips of the Romans didn’t shed His blood, He allowed His own blood to be shed. Yet, His blood covers us and makes us guiltless. There is nothing more condemnable than the senseless murder of an innocent person. No one on earth has ever been truly innocent except Jesus, and we shed His blood. It was for our sins that He died. It was by our hands that He was killed. There are other stories in the Bible of other seemingly innocent people being killed wrongly, Able for example. God said that his blood cried out for retribution, for justice even from the ground where his brother Cain had slain and buried him. We see in the book of Revelation that the saints who are killed for the Gospel cry out for their blood to be avenged as well. But Jesus’ blood, being more innocent than that of any other person, cries out for our forgiveness and imputes innocence to us. Usually when there is blood on a person’s hand they are guilty, yet with Jesus' blood on our hands He offers us forgiveness. We shed His blood and He invites us to be covered by His blood and have all of our sins cleansed, washed, erased. He makes us pure, righteous, and innocent before God with His blood which He allowed to be shed by us. One final note, the Passover meal had four different cups of wine that were drank at specific points throughout the meal. Each cup had a name, the name of the cup that Jesus used when He said “This is My blood” is the Cup of Redemption. Another Cup that shares the same name is the Wedding Cup. In Hebrew culture a man who desires a wife must go to his bride’s father and agree on a price to be paid to take the woman from her family and make her part of the man’s family. Once the price was agreed upon the man would go to his bride and offer her the cup, if she drank that was an acceptance of his proposal and they were legally married. With the cup the man said to his bride, I have agreed to pay this price for you, and I consider you to be more than worth it. Jesus says the same to us as He bids us drink from His blood. He agreed with the Father on the price that He paid to obtain us, He considers us to be more than worth His death on the cross, and because of His resurrection we can spend every moment of our life with Him.
