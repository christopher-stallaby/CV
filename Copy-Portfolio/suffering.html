Suffering

James 1:2 says, "Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds."

When Juan told me he was going to give Eduardo and I this opportunity to speak, I began asking God for something to say that would help us no matter where we are at in our walk with God or our walk in life. God told me, "You prepare something." So I began to work on something that God gave me a couple of months ago. So for the next couple of minutes I want to talk to you about suffering. Before I begin though I would like to encourage you, don't discount the message because of the messenger. I know I am younger than most of you, so most of you have more life experiences and have had to suffer through more than I have, but don't miss out what God would say to you because it appears I haven't suffered like you. Don't discount the message because of the messenger. God used a donkey once to give His message to the prophet Balaam, so I'm certain that God can use me. I'd like to set this up for a moment, James 1:2 says, "Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds." Ephesians 5:20 "Giving thanks always and for everything to God the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ." For those of you who don't know me, I have grown up in church. So I have a good history with the Bible, I've heard many verses and I've heard entire messages around these specific verses. Notice that James actually made that "counting everything joy" a command. He didn't say count it joy when you can, when you feel like it. He actually said, when you're suffering count it as joy. That giving thanks is also a command. So the Bible commands us to rejoice in suffering and even give thanks for it. That messes with me because it goes against what makes sense in the natural world. In the natural suffering is bad, but the Holy Spirit paints a different picture of suffering. When we read the book of Acts we see Peter and John preaching the gospel. They get arrested by the Sanhedrin, the very people that just two months earlier had put Jesus to death. This council beats them, then throws them back out into the street telling them not to preach Jesus anymore. Acts 5:41 records their actions as they left, having just been beaten... "Then they left the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the Name." So now not only do we have the commands to rejoice in suffering, and be thankful for these trials, we also have an example of when the Apostles did this. It would be one thing for Jesus to do this, but this is Peter and John, who didn't even defend Jesus at His arrest. These are real men, facing real suffering who left rejoicing. Something I didn't understand for a long time is how? How can anyone go through any type of suffering and rejoice because of it? Here's what God showed me, Romans 5:3-5 "Not only that, but we rejoice in our sufferings," I heard this and I thought to myself, Here we go again, God how am I ever supposed to do this? Well, the verse continued playing, because I was listening to this on audio, "Knowing that suffering produces endurance, and endurance produces character, and character produces hope, and hope does not put s to shame because God's love has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us." Suffering produces endurance, endurance produces character and character produces hope. Paul says that hope is Christ in us. (Colossians 1:27) The hope is Christ in us, so the character is His character, the endurance is the strength of that character, being able to withstand the trials of life and maintain the attitude of Jesus. All of this, brought about by suffering. You know, Jesus compares the kingdom of heaven to a seed. (Matthew 13:24, 31 Mark 4:26) What does a seed need if it is going to grow? It needs good ground, it needs sunlight and water and it needs seasons. Paul says the Word of God is like water in Ephesians 5:26, but there's another type of water mentioned in scripture, the water of suffering. Isaiah 59:19 says, "When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the Lord shall lift up a standard against him." Notice, it says when he comes in. So the standard isn't to keep him out, but to limit what he can do. So, the Holy Spirit limits the suffering we go through. So, the enemy and all his attacks, his arsenal of weapons to use against us is compared to water as well. There are seasons where we experience the life-giving water of the Word, like spring and summer. Then there are seasons in which we experience the testing, trying, proving waters of suffering like fall and winter. But this suffering does not come to destroy the life that God has given, but to strengthen it. Isaiah 54:16-17 I shared a couple of weeks ago that God is even in control of the forming process of the weapons. So the enemy is disarmed before he even launches an assault against us. The attacks that God allows, the suffering that comes is under His direct supervision. Never without His permission, and always with the plan to make us like Christ (Jeremiah 29:11, 2 Corinthians 3:18). The Holy Spirit is also the Master Craftsman, Ephesians 2:10 says we are "His workmanship." There are two types of water which are used in the growth process of this Seed of Christ that has been placed inside of us. The Water of the Word, and the water of suffering. The Holy Spirit knows exactly what is needed, and how much is needed. Notice that in Isaiah 59:19 it said that the Spirit of the Lord raised up a standard against the enemy when he came in, so that the attack of the enemy was limited by God. 1 Corinthians 10:13 "No temptation has overtaken you that is not common to man. God is faithful and He will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with temptations He will also provide the way of escape that you may be able to endure it." So the Spirit limits the suffering that the devil is allowed to bring us, Job also shows this. So, when we get saved, we receive Christ He puts inside of us everything we need in the form of a seed (2Corinthians 5:21, 2Peter 1:3) The good thing about this seed is its incorruptible, so this Seed never dies, (1 Peter 1:23). The water of the Word brings life to the seed inside of us, (John 6:63) and the water of suffering tests the strength of life, and the obedience and submission we have toward the seed of Christ in us. Even Jesus learned obedience through the things He suffered, Hebrews 5:8, and the servant is not greater than the Master, John 13:16. If He learned obedience through suffering, how much more will we? See, each water, whether it's the Word or the suffering, has one purpose. To bring about the strength of the character of Christ inside of us, and ultimately display that character. If we can come to view our suffering, whatever it may be a trial, a temptation, maybe its physical maybe its mental maybe its spiritual, if we can understand that everything that happens to me is allowed by God, with the intent to bring me closer to Him and make me more like Jesus then, even though our situations may not change, they won't have to change. Because then, everything that happens to me is ultimately for my good, whether it appears good or not (Romans 8:28). So, even the suffering that we go through is not by chance. It is designed and orchestrated by God, brought by the devil, but controlled by our Father who loves us so that we can be more like Jesus. When we see things through this filter, then even while emotions may run high or low we won't be blown off course, we won't be so easily discouraged, because it's controlled by God to bring us closer to Him.
