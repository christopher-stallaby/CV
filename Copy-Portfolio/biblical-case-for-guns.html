Biblical Grounds for the Second Amendment

Published on FL&J Blog

A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed. - American Constitution, Second Amendment

The Declaration of Independence which gave rise to the great United States of America states, “We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all men are… endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights…” The Constitution of the United States builds upon this and adds its Second Amendment to the list of “Rights” that the Founders believed to be “unalienable.” Simply put, those who drafted the Declaration of Independence along with those who wrote and ratified the Constitution of the United States believed that every person had the right to protect themselves in a manner that the individual saw fit. But, were they right? Is this an “unalienable” right that has been “endowed” from the “Creator”? During the exodus from Egypt and the wandering of the Israelites through the wilderness and into the promised land, they repeatedly encountered hostile forces that came against the Israelites to destroy them. Firstly, this happened in Exodus 14 when the Pharaoh of Egypt and his army pursued Israel to recapture them. In this specific instance, God delivered Israel by causing the Red Sea, which he had parted for Israel to cross, to close and drown the entire Egyptian army. Another instance in Exodus 17 in which the Amalekites attack the Israelites and instead of supernatural intervention this time the Lord’s plan was for the men to go out and fight (17:9). So God chooses to defend His people both supernaturally and through their own efforts. In Numbers 33, God instructs the people of Israel that they “must drive out all the people living [in the promised land]... Take possession of the land and settle in it, because I have given it to you to occupy (52-53).” Afterward He warns, “But if you fail to drive out the people who live in the land, those who remain will be like splinters in your eyes and thorns in your sides. They will harass you in the land where you live (55).” So, even though God specifically promised this land to Israel, and clearly could have supernaturally delivered it into their hand, He used their own efforts more often than His miraculous intervention. From this, one possible conclusion is that people have a right to forcefully insist on what God has given them and to aggressively defend that which belongs to them. Certainly, every person has received from God the gift of life. “In Him we live and move and have our being (Acts 17:28).” So, every human being has the responsibility to defend their life. This truth lays the foundation for the Second Amendment. Without an individual’s right or responsibility to defend themself, no such amendment would have been written because then the responsibility does not belong to the individual with whom this Amendment is chiefly concerned. The commands of God set forth in the Biblical histories of Israel provide a pattern for all peoples and nations to follow if they wish to live well and reap the maximum possible benefits from life. The Framers who wrote the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution of the United States were wise men who understood many of the Biblical principles that made Israel a successful nation. Using this understanding they formed a nation and a government that mirrored the patterns depicted in the Bible and built on them as best they could. “The right of the people to keep and bear Arms” based on the individual’s right to life and to defend that life is not only wise and useful to keep criminals and tyrannical governments in check, it is Biblical.
