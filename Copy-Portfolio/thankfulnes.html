Group Lead Notes

Thankfulness

Exodus 16:1-3,11-12 "The entire Israelite community departed from Elim and came to the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after they had left the land of Egypt. The entire Israelite community grumbled against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. The Israelites said to them, 'If only we had died by the Lord's hand in the land of Egypt, when we sat by pots of meat and ate all the bread we wanted. Instead, you have brought us into this wilderness to make this whole assembly die of hunger!'"... "The Lord spoke to Moses, 'I have heard the complaints of the Israelites. Tell them: At twilight you will eat meat, and in the morning you will eat bread until you are full. Then you will know that I am Yahweh your God.'" Psalm 100:4 "Enter His gates with thanksgiving, and His courts with praise! Give thanks to Him; bless His name."

It is interesting to me that in response to the words of the children of Israel that God does not say that He heard their prayers, rather He heard their complaints. Meaning, there is a difference in praying to God and complaining to Him. He still granted their request, which by all means was a reasonable one, after all they had no other source of food, it was right of them to ask of God. Yet, their actions were not asking or praying but complaining. We can see from Hebrews 3:7-11 that this particular generation which constantly complained to Moses and Aaron provoked God to wrath. Why is it though that complaining angers God? I think an answer is found in the second Scripture listed above. Because by giving thanks we enter His gates. From Jesus' teachings it is plain to see that there are at least two kingdoms at work in our world today. The kingdom of God, which Christ spoke of often and openly, and the kingdom of Satan (see Matthew 12:24-26, Luke 11:15-18). These two kingdoms are necessarily opposites. Light and darkness, life and death, are pictures used to describe them. If then it is our thankfulness that opens the gates of the kingdom of God to allow us entrance; then its opposite, complaining, will open the gates of the kingdom of Satan for out entrance there. Romans chapter 1 also deals with an issue of unthankfulness saying that even though people know God they choose not to be thankful and refuse to honor Him as God. This goes to show that complaining and unthankfulness are more than simple emotional reactions in the heat of a moment of something going wrong. At base, these actions are an assault on the character and person of God. Certainly feelings of frustration are natural when circumstances do not go as planned or expected, even our Lord felt frustration at times. Mark 9:19 "And He answered them, 'Oh faithless generation, how long am I to be with you? How long am I to bear with you? Bring him to Me.'" Meditating on this frustration though is what lends our tongue to speak words of complaining, which is the devil's praise. Then in that state you begin to hold in contempt the very gifts God gave you which you initially asked for, and which He in His goodness both promised and provided. This is what has happened to the children of Israel in the passage above. God foretold of their slavery, and their deliverance. During their exile in Egypt they cried out to God for their deliverance. But now in the wilderness, in the frustration of a perceived lack of something they needed they began to question God. They accused Him of devious motives, saying He had brought them out to kill them and even went so far as to wish they could be back in Egypt. This issue of being thankful or not is often overlooked or at least taken lightly. I don't think we can afford to do this, especially in light of this teaching and the Scripture which commands us to always give thanks "for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ..." (Ephesians 5:20 KJV)
