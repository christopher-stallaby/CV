Prayer: A Constant Conversation

Prayer is a topic that is covered fairly extensively in religious circles. I myself own three books on prayer (one is a collection of 5 books by E. M. Bounds all rolled into one volume so it is technically seven books). Still though, for normal people who go to church often and hear about the prayer lives of great men from days gone by, prayer can seem to be this lofty privilege that is reserved for the holy. Or prayer could be an honor bestowed on those who have the time to pray, and the discipline to pray more than anything else. But what about the rest of us?

What about those who work almost all of the daylight hours? What about the ones who do not feel they have the scriptural vocabulary to be able to pray well? We see verses like 1 Thessalonians 5:17 "Pray without ceasing" and 1 Timothy 2:8 "I desire that in every place the men should pray..." Jesus gave a parable on how to pray and that we should always pray, found in Luke 18:1-8. What is this prayer that we are supposed to be doing all the time, everywhere, without failing or becoming discouraged when it looks like we are not being heard? I believe prayer to be a simple conversation. Jesus laid out a guideline for prayer in Matthew 6:9. Yet, in practice He does not use the same words. He also seems to deviate from the guideline which He set forth for His disciples to follow. For example, in John 11:41, during the story of Lazarus' death and Jesus' resurrection of Lazarus, He says "Father, I thank You that You heard Me." First of all, what did Jesus say that the Father heard? This is the beginning of His prayer.. He continues to pray in verse 42 "I know that You always hear Me, but because of the crowd standing here I said this, so they may believe You have sent Me." Jesus is just casually talking with Father, as if they were together face to face. This is what prayer is: conversation with God. We do not have to have all the right words or phrases. We don't have to be perfectly still or on our knees or entirely undistracted by what is going on around us (though all this helps tremendously)! Simple conversation, as if every time we speak God is listening attentively. Hanging on every word we say, revelling in every thought and idea that we share with Him, this is the way God treats prayer. This is the way we should approach praying too! What an honor to be able to speak to Him and for Him to listen, hear, understand and respond to our hearts as we share with Him our lives, thoughts, feelings, doubts (yes doubts), fears, hopes and struggles. God is not disappointed by anything we would tell Him. He is a lover who longs to discover all that we are willing to share with Him. In a conversation though, both parties must be involved. If we only talk to God, without listening for His response, then it is not a conversation but a monologue, it must be dialogue with the Trinity. We listen while He speaks, we are attentive to what He says and revel as He shares the secrets of His heart with us. The examples of mighty prayer which we see in the Bible, such as Joshua telling the sun to stand still and it obeys, or Elijah rebuking the rain and for three years not a drop falls from the sky, or Jesus speaking to a storm and it stops immediately; these are not meant to intimidate us. Rather we should see this as an invitation to the kind of influence that is possible for those who pray. An important aspect of prayer that should be mentioned is this! God already knows everything we are thinking and feeling, yet He delights in our openness in telling Him everything. He will not embarrass or force us to be more intimate with Him than we are willing, but His desire is to be "closer than a brother" (Proverbs 18:24). Song of Solomon portrays Jesus as our heavenly Bridegroom, John the Baptist confirmed that this analogy does indeed speak of Jesus by referring to Him as such in John 3:29. With the first people in existence, Adam and Eve, God made a statement of the type of relationship He desires. After sin and the fall Adam and Eve clothed themselves, then after God gives discipline and curses their sin, He too clothes them better than they had clothed themselves. So God was capable of clothing these people any time that it pleased Him. Yet, when they were first created, they were naked. I asked the Lord one day why this was the case, why they were naked if He could have made clothes for them from the beginning. "Because you can't hide anything when you are naked." Came His response to my question. I was blown away!! From the beginning God has always desired a relationship, I knew that much before this conversation. But now I knew that God's desire was for intimate relationships, like husband and wife. Where there are no secrets, nothing hidden or held back. God desires true openness with us, without fear or shame. God is constantly with us, ready and willing to speak to us. He invites us to participate in an endless conversation. He desires intimacy in prayer while we tell Him everything. He cherishes each moment of time we choose to spend talking with Him. This is prayer!
